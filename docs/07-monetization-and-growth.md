# Monetization & Growth

## YouTube Partner Program (YPP) requirements

- 1,000 subscribers + 10M Shorts views in 90 days, **or** 4,000 valid public
  watch hours in 12 months (long-form) — Shorts-heavy strategy means the
  Shorts-views threshold is realistically the faster path here given the
  daily-shorts cadence.
- Once eligible: Shorts ads revenue share, long-form ads, channel
  memberships, Super Thanks. Apply as soon as eligible — there's no downside
  to applying early.

## Instagram monetization

- Reels bonuses/monetization availability varies by region/program status —
  check current eligibility in Professional Dashboard once follower/view
  thresholds are met. Less predictable than YouTube's program; treat IG
  primarily as a growth/distribution channel and YouTube as the primary
  revenue channel, at least initially.

## Cross-promotion to your other projects (per your "light CTA later" direction)

- **Phase 1 (now):** none. Build "Finest City Facts" as its own credible
  brand — a channel that immediately reads as a funnel to something else
  undermines the "love letter to the city" positioning that makes it work.
- **Phase 2 (post-traction, e.g. 5-10K followers):** a simple bio/link-in-bio
  mention is enough — "also behind [other project]" — no in-video CTA needed
  yet.
- **Phase 3 (established):** if your other projects are genuinely
  San-Diego-relevant (local business, real estate, tourism-adjacent, etc.),
  an occasional *organic* content tie-in works better than a CTA — e.g. a
  "hidden gems" video that naturally features something related, credited
  honestly, rather than an ad-read bolted onto an unrelated fact video.
  Whenever you're ready to define what those other projects are, this
  section can get specific about the actual integration points (affiliate
  links, a dedicated "brought to you by" segment, etc.) — flagging the slot
  for that now so it's a five-minute update later, not a rebuild.

## Other monetization paths (beyond ad revenue)

- **Merch** (you already have Shopify connected in this workspace) — a
  "Finest City" branded merch line is a very natural extension of this exact
  channel concept once there's an audience; the tooling to stand it up is
  already available when you're ready.
- **Local business sponsorships**: once the channel has real local reach, SD
  businesses (restaurants, tour companies, breweries) are a natural sponsor
  category — this is a "when the audience exists" item, not a launch item.
- **Affiliate**: San Diego travel-adjacent affiliate programs (hotels, tours)
  for the long-form "things to do" content specifically — lower priority
  than ad revenue/sponsorship given the audience is more local-pride than
  active-trip-planning, but worth testing once long-form is established.

## KPI tracking plan

Track weekly, log into `data/` (or a Notion dashboard — recommended once
volume makes CSVs unwieldy):

- **Leading indicators (check these first, they predict the rest):**
  retention/average-view-duration %, hook drop-off rate (first-3-second
  retention specifically, if the platform surfaces it).
- **Growth:** subscriber/follower net change, views, shares/saves (saves are
  a stronger IG signal than likes for this content type).
- **Content-level:** which pillar and which hook pattern is over/under
  performing — feed this back into `pipeline/topic_selector.py`'s pillar
  weighting and into the script-writer prompt's hook selection over time.
- **Business (once monetized):** RPM/CPM, YPP status, any
  sponsorship/affiliate revenue.

Review cadence: quick check daily (is anything broken/underperforming
badly), real trend review weekly, strategy-level review monthly (is the
pillar mix still right, is it time to phase out the review queue, is it time
to add TikTok, etc. — see docs/09 roadmap for the decision points).
